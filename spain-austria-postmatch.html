🧭 The Story — The Awakening Everyone Was Waiting For

For two and a half games, Spain had been all clean sheets and no conviction — winning without persuading anyone they were contenders. Against Austria, the fog lifted. La Roja settled at last into a coherent, flowing, ferociously dominant shape, ran their opponents ragged from the first whistle, and won 3-0 in a performance whose margin should have been far greater. It was, tellingly, Spain's first World Cup knockout victory since they lifted the trophy in 2010, and the first time since 1994 they have scored at least twice in a World Cup knockout match. More than the result, it was the manner: the intricate triangles, the trademark tiki-taka, the second and third waves of attack breaking on a drowning Austrian defence. Spain, at long last, have come to life — and the rest of the tournament should take note.

🧩 The settled shape — full width, layered waves

The transformation began with the team shape, which finally looked right. Cucurella and Baena down the left; Pedro Porro and a near-unstoppable, self-described "80% fit" Yamal down the right; Oyarzabal and Olmo through the centre; and Pedri operating just behind them all. This gave Spain genuine attacking width across the whole pitch and, crucially, a layered structure that produced wave after wave: when the first attack broke down, a second arrived, and then a third, because bodies were staggered at the right depths. Spain won the ball back with astonishing speed — frequently inside 12–13 seconds of losing it — and simply suffocated Austria. It was the most complete Spanish performance of the tournament by a distance.

🌊 The Shape — Full Width and the Three Waves

Spain · StructureLayered attackers produce relentless second and third waves
Wave 1 (front) Wave 2 / 3 support

The structure that finally clicked. The genius of the setup was its depth. With Baena and Cucurella providing width and overlap on the left, Yamal and the overlapping Porro on the right, Oyarzabal and Olmo occupying the centre, and Pedri floating behind, Spain attacked with a layered staircase of runners. When the first wave was repelled, the ball dropped to a second line arriving on the edge of the box, and behind them a third. Austria could never fully clear their lines because there was always another red shirt arriving. And the counter-pressing was ferocious: possession regained within twelve or thirteen seconds, again and again, so the waves simply kept coming. Austria, short of pace up front and unable to launch quick counters — Cubarsí's recovery speed snuffed out what little they mustered — were left to defend for their lives.

⚽ Goal 1 (36') — Oyarzabal turns home Cucurella's cross

Spain · 1-0The breakthrough the relentless pressure deserved
Cucurella's low cross Oyarzabal's finish

Relentless pressure, finally rewarded. The lead, when it came just after the half-hour, was overdue. Spain had already been denied — Cucurella slammed home a corner only for it to be chalked off for a foul on the keeper — and had spurned a stream of openings. The goal itself was pure quality: Marc Cucurella found a yard of space on the left, and picked out Mikel Oyarzabal near the penalty spot with a low cross. The Real Sociedad man made the first-time redirect look effortless — which it emphatically was not — to break the deadlock. It was scant reward for a first-half onslaught that also brought a Baena free-kick crashing off the crossbar and a blocked Yamal follow-up. On another night, Spain lead by four at the interval; Alexander Schlager's four fine saves alone kept it to one.

⏸️ Half-time — 1-0, but it should have been a rout

A deserved lead that flattered Austria. Spain had produced wave upon wave of attack, at least four excellent Austrian saves, a disallowed goal, and a Baena free-kick off the bar with Yamal denied on the rebound. Austria, pace-less up front and pinned in their own half, offered next to nothing. The only Spanish frustration was the scoreline — 1-0 did not remotely reflect the gulf. What followed after the restart would remove all doubt.

✨ The Yamal Show — Genius at 80 Per Cent

Spain · The TalismanDouble-marked all night, and still uncontainable
Yamal's horizontal dribble

Even at four-fifths fitness, the best show in town. By his own estimate only "80% fit," Lamine Yamal was still the most watchable player on the pitch. Austria did exactly as Rangnick had promised, marking him with two defenders — one of them Lima — for much of the night, and still it wasn't enough: Yamal stole the ball out from between his markers on nearly a dozen occasions. The signature moment came when he ran horizontally across the face of the goal, gliding past five Austrian defenders who could only watch in disbelief, the ball apparently magnetised to his boots. He was denied a deserved goal only by fine margins — a shot blocked here, an effort cleared off the line by Alaba there. That Spain could remove him with ten minutes to go, the game long won, and bring on Gavi, only underlined the luxury. A fit Yamal in the latter rounds is a genuinely frightening prospect.

🎨 The Second Half — Tiki-Taka Reborn

If the first half was dominance, the second was a statement of identity. Spain began the second half brilliantly, and for a spell made it literally impossible for Austria to escape their own half — it took the Austrians a full twelve minutes after the restart to so much as reach the Spanish half. The intricate triangles, the one-touch combinations, the patient probing that shifts the opposition side to side until a gap yawns open: the trademark tiki-taka was all there, and it was glorious. Pedri, so out of sorts earlier in the tournament, visibly grew in confidence — operating a touch deeper than his natural station, with so many bodies ahead of him, he began to dictate. Olmo, the game-changer in the No.10 role, was desperately unlucky, several of his shots smothered by desperate Austrian blocks. When Rangnick threw on five attacking changes and the game opened up, it merely gave Spain more room to torment. The pressure could only end one way.

⚽ Goal 2 (66') — Porro caps the team goal of the tournament

Spain · 2-0A flowing move across the width, finished by the overlapping full-back
Passing chain & byline cross Porro's header

A goal that distilled everything great about the performance. The second was the reward for all that patient dominance — and for many watching, a candidate for the team goal of the tournament. Spain worked the ball across the full width of the pitch in a sustained sequence of intricate passes just in front of the box, shifting Austria from one side to the other until the gap appeared. Cucurella and then Baena carried it left; Baena reached the byline and whipped in a pinpoint cross; and Pedro Porro, the right-back, arrived from deep at the perfect moment to power a header beyond Schlager. It was Porro's first international goal on his 20th cap — and a fitting one, because it was manufactured entirely by the collective. The full-back finishing a move built on the opposite flank was the perfect emblem of a team playing as one flowing organism again.

🛡️ The Unsung Truth — This Spanish Defence Is Superb

Spain · The FoundationClean sheets built on aerial timing, work-rate and a modern balance
Austria's aerial ball Headed clear / covered

Amid all the flair, the foundation is a back line that simply does not concede. With so much happening at the other end, it is easy to overlook that Spain have now kept a clean sheet in all four matches — only the second side, after Mexico, to do so in this tournament. When Austria turned to their tall substitutes and started launching aerial balls into the box, Spain answered with impeccable timing and perfectly judged jumps to meet everything in the air. And the deeper truth is about balance: the workhorses often forgotten in the glow of the attack — Rodri and Laporte screening and sweeping in front of and behind the line, and Cucurella, whose defensive diligence matches his attacking supply yet who somehow stays fresh for the full ninety. This is the great evolution from the purist, sometimes-brittle tiki-taka of the Luis Enrique era to De la Fuente's more complete, two-way side. The romance up front is real — but it is built on a defence that is quietly one of the best at the tournament.

⚽ Goal 3 (89') — Oyarzabal's second, the exclamation mark

The scoring was rounded off in the dying minutes, and again it flowed from the left. Cucurella — inevitably — raced into space and slid a perfectly weighted pass into the box, and Oyarzabal, beating the offside trap, calmly stroked his second of the night into the corner. It was his fourth of the tournament and, remarkably, his 17th goal in 16 Spain starts — a striker quietly enjoying a prolific competition. By then the game had long been an exhibition; the third was merely the number the performance had always deserved. Between Oyarzabal's brace, Porro's header and a supporting cast that changed nothing when the substitutes arrived, Spain's bench strength — Gavi on for Yamal, Ferran Torres for Baena, Merino for Olmo, all slotting in seamlessly — was its own quiet warning to the rest of the field.

📋 The Performers

Mikel Oyarzabal — Two goals, ruthless. A brace of composed finishes; four for the tournament, 17 in 16 starts. The false-9 experiment is paying off handsomely.
Lamine Yamal — Unplayable at 80%. Double-marked all night, stole the ball a dozen times, glided past five men in one run. Unlucky not to score (cleared off the line). A frightening watch.
Marc Cucurella — Two assists & a full shift. Supplied both Oyarzabal goals, reached the byline at will, and did all his defensive work while staying fresh for 90. The model modern full-back.
Dani Olmo — The game-changer. His presence in the No.10 role transformed Spain's penetration. Desperately unlucky — several shots blocked. The missing piece found.
Pedri — Growing into it. Flashes of the real Pedri, played slightly deeper amid so many bodies ahead. Grew in confidence in the second half. Not yet peak, but trending up.
Pedro Porro — First international goal. A perfectly timed run from deep to head home the pick of the goals. An attacking threat down the right all night.

🔮 The Future — A Contender Emerges, on the Wrong Side of the Draw

Make no mistake: this was the night Spain announced themselves as genuine contenders. The stuttering, unconvincing side of the group stage is gone; in its place stands a team that has rediscovered its identity, settled its best shape, and paired mesmeric attacking football with a miserly, well-drilled defence. When Olmo pulls the strings behind Oyarzabal, when Yamal is fit and flying, when Pedri finds his rhythm and Cucurella and Porro bomb on from full-back, Spain look like a side capable of winning the whole thing. The four-from-four clean-sheet record is the platform; the flair is the ceiling. La Roja are coming to the boil at exactly the right time.

The one shadow is the draw. Spain sit on the same side as France — the tournament's other team playing football from another planet — which means the two juggernauts are on a collision course before any final. It is, frankly, a shame that a resurgent Spain and a rampant France could meet in a semi-final rather than the showpiece; one of these thrilling sides will knock the other out too early. But that is a worry for later rounds. First comes a Round-of-16 meeting, most likely with Portugal (or Croatia), in Dallas on Monday.

Portugal would be a formidable step up — a squad laden with talent and, in Cristiano Ronaldo, a serial matchwinner, even if their own tournament has been curiously blunt in front of goal (a goalless draw with Colombia among their group results). The tactical battle would be fascinating: Spain's possession dominance and half-space penetration against Portugal's individual quality and threat in transition. On tonight's evidence, Spain will fear no one. If Olmo keeps unlocking defences, if Yamal climbs from 80% toward full fitness, and if Pedri completes his return to form, La Roja have the tools to go all the way — and to give France, should they meet, the game of the tournament. The awakening is real. Vamos España.

📊 The Last Word

A 3-0 that could, and perhaps should, have been six — and a performance that changes the complexion of Spain's tournament entirely. After weeks of winning without convincing, La Roja finally settled into their best shape and produced the flowing, relentless, joyous football that has been their promise all along: full width, layered waves, ferocious counter-pressing, an 80%-fit Yamal still bewitching five men at a time, Olmo unlocking the No.10 role, and a team goal for the ages finished by Porro. Beneath the flair sits a defence that has not conceded all tournament — the modern balance that separates this side from the purist teams of the past. The awakening could hardly be better timed. Spain are contenders now, and only the cruelty of the bracket — sharing France's half — tempers the excitement. First, Portugal await. On this evidence, La Roja will relish it. Vamos España.
